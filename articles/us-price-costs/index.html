The costs to include in a US price: duty, carrier fee, Etsy fees

Last checked: 14 November 2026.

When you sell to a US buyer from another country and prepay the duty, three costs sit between your price and what you keep. Miss one and each US sale quietly earns less than a home sale.

1. Import duty

A percentage of the declared value, set by what the item is and where it was made. Some goods are free of the normal duty; additional tariffs may still apply on top, depending on the country. Rates change, so write down the date you checked.

2. The carrier's handling fee

Many carriers charge a fee for paying duty on your behalf. It is usually a fixed amount per parcel, not a percentage. Etsy's own calculator does not include it. Check your carrier's current price list. If you often sell several items in one parcel, the fee is shared between them.

3. Etsy's fees on the higher price

If you fold duty into the item price, Etsy's percentage fees apply to that larger price too. So raising the price by exactly the duty amount is not enough: part of the increase goes to fees.

Putting them together

US price = (home price × (1 − fee rate) + carrier fee) ÷ (1 − fee rate − duty rate)

Example with made-up numbers: home price £30, fees 10%, duty 10%, carrier fee £3. The US price comes to £37.50. Of the £7.50 difference, £3.75 is duty, £3.00 is the carrier fee and £0.75 is extra Etsy fees on the higher price.

Notice that the increase is 25%, although the duty rate is 10%. That gap is why "add the duty percentage" under-prices.

A checklist before you set US prices

  • Do I know the tariff code for each product type, or have I asked a broker?
  • Have I checked the normal rate and any additional tariff for my country, and noted the date?
  • Do I know my carrier's duty-handling fee?
  • Do I know my real fee rate, including payment processing?
  • Is duty also charged on my shipping price? If so, the sum needs that too.
  • Have I priced each variation, not just the listing?

What this leaves out

Currency-conversion costs, VAT differences, duties charged by weight or per item, and US customs processing fees.
