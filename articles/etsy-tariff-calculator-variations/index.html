Why Etsy's tariff calculator doesn't work on variation listings, and what to do

Last checked: 14 November 2026.

If you sell from outside the United States and your listings have options at different prices, such as ring sizes, print sizes or bundle sizes, you have probably found that Etsy's US tariff calculator won't open for them. This is not a fault in your shop. It is how the tool works at the moment.

What Etsy says

Etsy's help page for the US Estimated Tariffs Calculator says: "It's currently unavailable for listings with price variations." It also says its estimates "reflect official government tariffs and do not include additional fees your carrier may charge", and that "Etsy does not guarantee the accuracy or reliability of these estimates." The calculator is used inside a single listing's editor, so it handles one listing at a time.

So for a listing with priced variations, you have to work out the US price for each option yourself.

The sum, one variation at a time

The aim is simple: a sale to a US buyer should leave you with the same money as a sale at home.

For each variation you need four numbers:

  1. Home price of that variation.
  2. Fee rate: Etsy's fees as a share of the price. Use your own figure from your payment account.
  3. Duty rate for your product and country, as a percentage. This is the hard part; see "Where the duty rate comes from" below.
  4. Carrier's duty-handling fee per parcel, if your carrier charges one.

Then:

US price = (home price × (1 − fee rate) + carrier fee) ÷ (1 − fee rate − duty rate)

Why divide, and not just add the duty on top? Because the duty is charged on the higher US price, and Etsy's fees are charged on it too. Adding duty to the home price leaves you short.

A worked example (made-up numbers)

A print listing with three sizes priced £24, £30 and £36. Assume fees of 10%, duty of 10% and a £3 carrier fee. These numbers are made up for illustration; yours will differ.

VariationHome priceUS price
Small£24.00£30.75
Medium£30.00£37.50
Large£36.00£44.25

Check the middle one: at £37.50, fees take £3.75 and duty takes £3.75, and the carrier takes £3. That leaves £27.00, the same as a £30 home sale after 10% fees.

A spreadsheet layout that works

One row per variation, with these columns: listing, variation, home price, fee rate, duty rate, carrier fee, US price. Put the formula in the last column and copy it down. When a rate changes, change one cell and every price updates. Keep the date you last checked the duty rate at the top of the sheet.

Where the duty rate comes from

The rate depends on what the item is (its tariff code), where it was made, and any additional tariffs in force for that country. The official source for US rates is the Harmonized Tariff Schedule, which you can search by keyword. Additional tariffs are published in the Federal Register and can change. If you are unsure which code fits your product, a customs broker can tell you.

Limits of this method

It does not cover duty charged on shipping, currency-conversion costs, VAT differences between home and export sales, or duties charged per kilogram or per item. It is an estimate, not customs advice, and you remain responsible for what you declare.
