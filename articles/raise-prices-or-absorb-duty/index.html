Should I raise my US prices or absorb the duty?

Last checked: 16 November 2026.

Absorbing duty costs you the duty plus your carrier's fee on every US sale. Raising the price to cover it means raising it by more than the duty rate. Which you choose is a business decision; this article shows what each one costs.

All figures below are made up to show the sum: marketplace fees of 10%, duty of 10% and a carrier fee of £3 for prepaying duty. Yours will differ.

Option 1: absorb it

You leave your price alone and pay the duty and the carrier's fee yourself.

On a £30 item, a home sale leaves you £27.00 after fees. A US sale at the same price leaves you £27.00 minus £3.00 duty minus the £3.00 carrier fee: £21.00. You earn £6.00 less on each US sale.

Option 2: raise the price so your income stays the same

The price that leaves you the same £27.00 is not £30 plus 10%. Duty is charged on the higher price, and fees are too. The sum is:

US price = (home price × (1 − fee rate) + carrier fee) ÷ (1 − fee rate − duty rate)

For the £30 item that gives £37.50, an increase of 25%. How the sum works.

Option 3: split it

Any price between the two shares the cost with the buyer. At £34, you keep £24.20: £2.80 less than a home sale, and the buyer pays £4 more.

Three price points

The fixed carrier fee weighs most on cheaper items. That is why a single percentage across a whole shop over-prices some listings and under-prices others, and why listings with priced variations need a price for each variation.

Things only you can judge

  • How much of your income comes from US buyers.
  • Whether your buyers compare you with sellers inside the US, who have no duty to cover.
  • Whether you would rather sell fewer items at full income or more at less.

One thing is not optional: since 9 July, Etsy expects the duty to be prepaid and built into what US buyers pay. What Etsy's announcement says. The choice is about your price, not about whether duty is paid.
