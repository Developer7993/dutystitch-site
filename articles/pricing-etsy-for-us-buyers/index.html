How do I price my Etsy listings for US buyers?

Last checked: 16 November 2026.

Work out, for each listing and each priced variation, the US price that leaves you the same money as a sale at home, then enter it as the price US buyers see. This guide walks through the whole job and links to a detailed article for each step.

Key facts

  • Etsy expects sellers outside the US to build duties and import fees into what US buyers pay upfront, using a shipping option that prepays them. Etsy's announcement, checked 14 November 2026.
  • Etsy says of its own tariff calculator, "It's currently unavailable for listings with price variations", and that its estimates "do not include additional fees your carrier may charge". Etsy Help, checked 14 November 2026.
  • The duty-free allowance for small parcels no longer applies. What changed.

Step 1: know the four costs

A US sale has to cover the duty, your carrier's fee for prepaying it, and the extra marketplace fees charged on the higher price, and still leave you what a home sale does. The costs to include in a US price explains each one.

Step 2: do the sum

US price = (home price × (1 − fee rate) + carrier fee) ÷ (1 − fee rate − duty rate)

With made-up figures of 10% fees, 10% duty and a £3 carrier fee, a £30 item needs a US price of £37.50. That is 25% more, although the duty is 10%. Adding the duty percentage on top always leaves you short.

Step 3: price every variation

If a listing has options at different prices, each option needs its own US price, and Etsy's calculator will not do it for you. A method for variation listings, with a spreadsheet layout.

Step 4: decide how much to pass on

You can raise the price to keep your income the same, absorb the cost, or split it. Raise US prices or absorb the duty? shows what each costs at three price points. A fixed carrier fee weighs most on cheaper items, so one percentage across a whole shop over-prices some listings and under-prices others.

Step 5: round sensibly

Round up, never down, or the rounding eats the margin you just worked out. If your prices end in .95 or .99 at home, use the next such price above the exact figure.

Step 6: keep a dated record

Write down, for each product type, the tariff code you used, the duty rate, your carrier's fee and the date you checked them. When a rate changes you will know which prices to redo.

Step 7: check before you ship

Pricing is only half of it. The parcel has to go on a service that prepays the duty, with a customs description that matches what you priced. A pre-shipping checklist. If it goes wrong, this is what happens.

What this guide cannot tell you

  • Your duty rate. It depends on what the item is and where it was made. The official source for US rates is the Harmonized Tariff Schedule; a customs broker can advise if you are unsure which line fits.
  • Your carrier's fee. Ask your carrier.
  • Whether duty is charged on your shipping price as well. Ask your carrier; if it is, the sum needs that too.

A starting point for your own sheet

Your listings can be downloaded from Etsy as a file to build a price sheet from. How to download it and what is in it.
